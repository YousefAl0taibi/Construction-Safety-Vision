## Confidence-threshold trade-off

The fixed-threshold study uses class-aware matching at IoU `0.50` on all 141 held-out test images:

| Confidence | Macro precision | Macro recall | Macro F1 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.20 | 0.6364 | 0.5500 | 0.5448 |
| 0.35 | 0.7402 | 0.4855 | 0.5219 |
| 0.50 | 0.8638 | 0.4505 | 0.5116 |

These are macro averages sampled at explicit confidence thresholds, while `metrics.json` contains
the standard Ultralytics summary, so the two sets of Precision and Recall values should not be
compared as if they used the same averaging procedure. Moving from `0.35` to `0.20` increases macro
recall by 0.0645 and decreases macro precision by 0.1038. A recall-first, human-reviewed screening
workflow may prefer that trade-off; an unattended alerting system may not.

The `no_boots` curve has zero recall at all three thresholds. Its recorded precision of `1.0` is a
zero-prediction interpolation artefact and must not be read as good performance.

## Error analysis

The raw counts in the committed confusion matrix separate the test-set errors into three groups:

| Error type | Count |
|---|---:|
| Ground-truth objects predicted as background (omissions) | 351 |
| Predictions with no matched ground-truth object (background false positives) | 225 |
| Confusions between two foreground classes | 8 |

Omissions are therefore the largest recorded error group. `Person` accounts for 38 missed
ground-truth objects. The weakest class-level result is `no_boots`: it has 0 correct matches and all
23 of its ground-truth instances were assigned to background.

These counts identify *where* the detector fails, but not *why*. In particular, the matrix does not
prove that object size caused the omissions. Establishing a small-object effect would require
stratifying the test results by bounding-box area (and separately checking factors such as lighting
and occlusion).

## Training-label balance

The 1,132 training images have 1,132 matching label files containing 9,098 instances. Ten extra
label files without matching training images are excluded, and no training image is missing its
label file. `no_boots` is the smallest class with 88 instances (0.97% of all instances), versus
1,235 `boots` and 1,770 `Person` instances. This 14.0× imbalance relative to `boots` is a plausible
contributor to the zero test recall for `no_boots`, but establishing causation would require a
controlled retraining or resampling experiment.

## Video runtime benchmark

`saudi-video-benchmark.json` records a real-video measurement over 720 frames of the documented
Saudi airfield-repair clip. On the recorded CPU run, mean inference was `181.4 ms` and the complete
decode, inference, tracking, annotation, and MP4-encoding pipeline ran at `4.60 FPS`. This is below
the source rate of 24 FPS and is explicitly an offline result. GIF creation occurs after the timed
section. Temporary track-ID and helmet-status counts are heuristic per-frame outputs, not unique
people, incidents, or accuracy measurements.

## Saudi still/video status comparison

At confidence `0.35`, the 15 approved Saudi still images contain 75 detected people: 47 `OK`, 27
`UNKNOWN`, and one `NO_HELMET`. The corresponding shares are 62.7%, 36.0%, and 1.3%. The video has
280 `OK`, 671 `UNKNOWN`, and zero `NO_HELMET` across 951 person-frames. These are different counting
units over a small, related collection, so the comparison is qualitative rather than an accuracy
estimate. It is consistent with domain, scale, and visibility differences but cannot distinguish
their effects from detector class coverage.

The official dataset documentation categorises `none` as a generic “Other” class but does not
define it as a missing-helmet label. The rule engine therefore ignores it as helmet evidence and
keeps those cases `UNKNOWN`; only explicit `no_helmet` evidence can produce `NO_HELMET`.
